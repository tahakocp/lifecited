# 6. Mental health and crisis

### 1. If you're worried about someone, ask directly about suicide
- Cost: an awkward question.
- Gain: a review of the research found no significant increase in suicidal thoughts from being asked; talking about it may reduce them.
- Grade: B
- Source: [Dazzi et al. 2014, Does asking about suicide and related behaviours induce suicidal ideation? (review, abstract)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24998511/)
  > None found a statistically significant increase in suicidal ideation among participants asked about suicidal thoughts.
  > Our findings suggest acknowledging and talking about suicide may in fact reduce, rather than increase suicidal ideation
- Note: this was a literature review, not a meta-analysis. Asking does not replace getting them to professional help.

### 2. Keep a crisis line number for your country
- Cost: one search, now, before you need it.
- Gain: suicide is the second leading cause of death at ages 15–29; free, confidential helplines exist in most countries.
- Grade: C
- Source: [WHO, Suicide (fact sheet)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/suicide)
  > Suicide is the second leading cause of death among 15–29-year-olds.
- Source: [Find A Helpline (directory of helplines by country)](https://findahelpline.com/)
  > Helplines (also known as hotlines or crisis lines) provide immediate crisis counseling, emotional support and information – for free.
- Note: Find A Helpline is a private directory, not a government source. If someone is in immediate danger, call your emergency number.

### 3. Depression is treatable; most people never get treatment
- Cost: a visit to a doctor or a counsellor.
- Gain: effective treatment exists for mild, moderate and severe depression, yet only about one third of people with depression in high-income countries receive care.
- Grade: B
- Source: [WHO, Depressive disorder (fact sheet)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/depression)
  > There is effective treatment for mild, moderate and severe depression.
  > In high-income countries, only about one third of people with depression receive mental health treatment
