# 4. Money and scams

Where ordinary people lose the most money for the least reason: interest they did not need to pay, fees that compound, and scams that work by pressure. The sources are US and UK regulators; the rules behind them apply almost everywhere.

### 1. Pay the full credit card balance every month
- Cost: discipline. Turn on automatic full payment if your bank offers it.
- Gain: on most cards, paying the full balance by the due date means you pay no interest on purchases.
- Grade: C
- Source: [CFPB, What is a credit card interest rate?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-credit-card-interest-rate-what-does-apr-mean-en-44/)
  > On most cards, you can avoid paying interest on purchases if you pay your balance in full each month by the due date.

### 2. Check fund fees before you invest
- Cost: ten minutes reading the fee table.
- Gain: fees come out every year and compound, so small differences grow over decades.
- Grade: C
- Source: [FINRA, Mutual fund fees and expenses](https://www.finra.org/investors/investing/investment-products/mutual-funds/fees-expenses)
  > Even small differences in expenses might make a big difference in your return over time.
- Note: our own arithmetic, not from the source: 10,000 growing 6% a year for 30 years ends at about 57,000; at 5% (one point lost to fees) it ends at about 43,000.

### 3. Pressure to decide today means no
- Cost: saying "I'll call you back" and hanging up.
- Gain: real firms do not push you to invest on the spot; urgency, secrecy and flattery are the standard scam signs.
- Grade: C
- Source: [Report Fraud (UK police, formerly Action Fraud), Investment fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/investment-fraud)
  > Remember, legitimate organisations will never pressure you into investing on the spot.
- Source: [FCA, Protect yourself from scams](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/protect-yourself-scams)
  > Do you feel pressured to act quickly? Scammers might offer you a bonus or discount if you invest quickly, or they may say the opportunity is only available for a short time.
  > Is the offer exclusively for you? Scammers might claim that you’ve been specially chosen for an investment opportunity, and it should be kept a secret.

### 4. Check the firm on the regulator's register
- Cost: five minutes.
- Gain: if the firm is not regulated, you may have no compensation scheme or ombudsman when things go wrong.
- Grade: C
- Source: [Report Fraud (UK police, formerly Action Fraud), Investment fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/investment-fraud)
  > If you deal with a firm (or individual) that isn’t regulated, you may not be covered by the Financial Ombudsman Service (FOS) if things go wrong
- Note: every country has its own register (SEC/FINRA BrokerCheck in the US, FCA in the UK, ESMA lists in the EU). Use the phone number on the register, not the one in the email.

### 5. "We can recover your lost money" is the second scam
- Cost: nothing.
- Gain: after a fraud, scammers call back offering recovery for a fee; hang up.
- Grade: C
- Source: [Report Fraud (UK police, formerly Action Fraud), Investment fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/investment-fraud)
  > Fraudsters sometimes re-establish contact with previous victims claiming that they can help them recover lost money, this is just a secondary scam.

### 6. The regulator will never ask you to move money
- Cost: nothing.
- Gain: people pretending to be the regulator ask for transfers, PINs and passwords; the real one never does.
- Grade: C
- Source: [FCA, Protect yourself from scams](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/protect-yourself-scams)
  > Beware of scammers pretending to be from the FCA. We’d never ask you to transfer money to us and we’d never ask for your bank account PINs and passwords.
