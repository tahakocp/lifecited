# 5. Online safety

Five habits that block most attacks on ordinary people. All of them are free.

### 1. Use a password manager and long, unique passwords
- Cost: an afternoon to set it up.
- Gain: a manager makes long, random, unique passwords practical; CISA suggests at least 16 characters.
- Grade: C
- Source: [CISA, Use strong passwords](https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world/use-strong-passwords)
  > Create long, random, unique passwords with a password manager for safer accounts.
  > At least 16 characters—longer is stronger!
- Note: start with your email account. Whoever controls your email can reset every other account.
- Source: [NCSC, Top tips for staying secure online](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/top-tips-for-staying-secure-online)
  > Protect your email by using a strong and separate password

### 2. Turn on two-step verification
- Cost: a few seconds per new login.
- Gain: a second factor blocks many common attacks even when the password has leaked.
- Grade: C
- Source: [CISA, Require multifactor authentication (small business guide)](https://www.cisa.gov/audiences/small-and-medium-businesses/secure-your-business/require-multifactor-authentication)
  > Strong passwords help, but they are no longer enough to keep accounts and systems safe when used alone.
  > Implementing MFA into your policy is a simple, effective step that can block many common cyberattacks and significantly reduce the risk of account compromise.
- Note: use an authenticator app or a passkey where the site offers one; CISA calls for phishing-resistant methods.

### 3. Turn on automatic updates
- Cost: a restart now and then.
- Gain: updates fix security holes that attackers already know about.
- Grade: C
- Source: [CISA, Update software](https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world/update-software)
  > Fix security risks by installing updates and turning on automatic updates.

### 4. Back up what you can't lose
- Cost: a cloud plan or an external drive.
- Gain: a backup is the only fix for ransomware, theft and a dead phone.
- Grade: C
- Source: [NCSC, Always back up your most important data](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/top-tips-for-staying-secure-online/always-back-up-your-most-important-data)
  > A backup is a copy of your important data that's stored in a separate safe location
  > When the removable media isn't in use, it's important that you disconnect it.
- Note: test a restore once. A backup you have never restored is a guess. Keep one copy unplugged, because ransomware can reach a drive that stays attached.

### 5. Treat surprise messages that ask you to act as phishing
- Cost: going to the site yourself instead of clicking.
- Gain: phishing works through urgency and links; open the app or type the address yourself.
- Grade: C
- Source: [CISA, Recognize and report phishing](https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world/recognize-and-report-phishing)
  > Urgent or emotionally appealing language, especially messages that claim dire consequences for not responding immediately
  > However, if you think it could be real, don't click on any link or call any number in the message.
- Note: perfect grammar is no longer a sign that a message is real. CISA says AI-written phishing now has none of the old spelling mistakes.
- Source: [CISA, Recognize and report phishing](https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world/recognize-and-report-phishing)
  > in the era of artificial intelligence (AI) some emails will now have perfect grammar and spelling
