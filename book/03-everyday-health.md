# 3. Everyday health

Habits with large, well-measured effects. Nothing here is a diet or a supplement plan.

### 1. Move at least 150 minutes a week
- Cost: about 20 minutes a day of brisk walking or similar.
- Gain: people who are not active enough have a 20–30% higher risk of death than people who are.
- Grade: B
- Source: [WHO, Physical activity (fact sheet)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity)
  > People who are insufficiently active have a 20–30% increased risk of death compared to people who are sufficiently active.
  > they do not meet the global recommendations of at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity physical activity per week.

### 2. Sleep at least 7 hours
- Cost: going to bed earlier.
- Gain: the US recommendation for adults is at least 7 hours a night.
- Grade: B
- Source: [CDC, Sleep facts and stats (adults)](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/data-research/facts-stats/adults-sleep-facts-and-stats.html)
  > The recommended amount of sleep for adults is at least 7 hours each day.

### 3. Eat less than a teaspoon of salt a day
- Cost: fewer processed foods, less salt at the table.
- Gain: adults average 11 g of salt a day, more than double the WHO limit of 5 g; too much sodium was linked to 1.7 million deaths a year.
- Grade: B
- Source: [WHO, Sodium reduction (fact sheet)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/sodium-reduction)
  > The global mean intake of adults was 4278 mg/day sodium (equivalent to 11 g/day salt) in 2021
  > the World Health Organization recommendation for adults of less than 2000 mg/day sodium (equivalent to < 5 g/day salt, or approximately one teaspoon).
  > An estimated 1.7 million deaths each year were associated with consuming too much sodium in 2023
- Note: bread, processed meat, snacks and sauces carry much of it, not only the salt shaker.
- Source: [WHO, Sodium reduction (fact sheet)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/sodium-reduction)
  > It is often found in high amounts in processed foods such as breads, processed meat, and snack foods

### 4. Wash hands with soap for 20 seconds
- Cost: twenty seconds.
- Gain: hand-washing promotion cuts diarrhoea episodes by about 30% in trials; scrub for at least 20 seconds, or use sanitiser with at least 60% alcohol.
- Grade: A
- Source: [Ejemot-Nwadiaro et al. 2021, Hand-washing promotion for preventing diarrhoea (Cochrane review, abstract)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33539552/)
  > Hand-washing promotion probably reduces diarrhoea episodes in both child day-care centres in high-income countries and among communities living in LMICs by about 30%.
- Source: [CDC, About handwashing](https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/about/index.html)
  > Scrub your hands for at least 20 seconds.
  > If soap and water are not readily available, use a hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol to clean your hands.

### 5. Don't ask for antibiotics for a cold or flu
- Cost: nothing.
- Gain: antibiotics do not work on viruses, and resistance is already common: about 1 in 6 lab-confirmed bacterial infections resisted antibiotics in 2023.
- Grade: B
- Source: [CDC, Antibiotic use: the basics](https://www.cdc.gov/antibiotic-use/about/index.html)
  > Antibiotics DO NOT work on viruses.
  > Colds and runny noses, even if the mucus is thick, yellow or green
- Source: [WHO, Antimicrobial resistance (fact sheet)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/antimicrobial-resistance)
  > Approximately 1 in 6 laboratory-confirmed bacterial infections worldwide were resistant to antibiotics in 2023.

### 6. Paracetamol: never more than 8 tablets in 24 hours
- Cost: reading the label of every cold remedy.
- Gain: the adult limit is 8 tablets of 500mg in 24 hours, at least 4 hours apart (4 tablets if they are 1g); many flu remedies also contain paracetamol.
- Grade: C
- Source: [NHS, Paracetamol for adults](https://www.nhs.uk/medicines/paracetamol-for-adults/)
  > Paracetamol tablets and capsules for adults usually contain 500mg of paracetamol.
  > 500mg or 1g tablets and capsules
  > do not take more than 8 tablets or capsules in 24 hours
  > leave at least 4 hours between each dose
  > If you're taking these, you should only take one at a time and do not take more than 4 tablets in 24 hours.
- Note: get medical advice after taking too much, even if you feel fine.
- Source: [NHS, Paracetamol for adults](https://www.nhs.uk/medicines/paracetamol-for-adults/)
  > You should also avoid taking paracetamol at the same time as other medicines containing paracetamol, for example, cold and flu remedies.
  > it's important to get medical advice if you take too much as it can sometimes damage your liver.

### 7. Turn the volume down
- Cost: nothing.
- Gain: more than 1 billion young adults risk permanent hearing loss from unsafe listening.
- Grade: B
- Source: [WHO, Deafness and hearing loss (fact sheet)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/deafness-and-hearing-loss)
  > Over 1 billion young adults are at risk of permanent, avoidable hearing loss due to unsafe listening practices.
- Note: hearing loss from noise does not come back.

### 8. Cut sun exposure, not just sunburn
- Cost: shade, a hat, sunscreen.
- Gain: UV caused about 325 000 melanomas and 1.2 million other skin cancers in 2020.
- Grade: B
- Source: [WHO, Ultraviolet radiation (fact sheet)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/ultraviolet-radiation)
  > Excessive exposure to UVR caused around 1.2 million new cases of non-melanoma skin cancers (SCC and BCC) and 325 000 melanomas of the skin, and 64 000 premature deaths from non-melanoma and 57 000 melanomas of the skin in the year 2020.

### 9. Don't make 55-hour weeks normal
- Cost: some income or career speed, for some people.
- Gain: working 55 hours or more a week is linked to a 35% higher risk of stroke and a 17% higher risk of dying from heart disease, compared with 35 to 40 hours.
- Grade: B
- Source: [WHO and ILO, Long working hours increasing deaths from heart disease and stroke (2021)](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2021-long-working-hours-increasing-deaths-from-heart-disease-and-stroke-who-ilo)
  > working 55 or more hours per week is associated with an estimated 35% higher risk of a stroke and a 17% higher risk of dying from ischemic heart disease, compared to working 35-40 hours a week.
- Note: this is from observational data. It shows a link, not that every long week harms everyone.

### 10. Hot days: plan cool hours
- Cost: a few hours in a cool place, water, checking on older neighbours.
- Gain: heat stress is a leading cause of weather-related death; a few hours a day somewhere cool helps the body recover.
- Grade: C
- Source: [WHO, Heat and health (fact sheet)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/climate-change-heat-and-health)
  > Heat stress is a leading cause of weather-related deaths
  > Spend 2–3 hours during the day in a cool place.
